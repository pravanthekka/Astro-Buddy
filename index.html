    Project story

    Team The Sibling Circuit · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Challenge: build health monitoring software for astronauts on long missions. Add: team member names

    What we built

    A calm health companion for astronauts. A wearable band and a radiation badge send readings automatically, so the crew never has to type anything. The software watches quietly. When a reading drifts, it explains what is happening in plain words and gives clear next steps. If something is urgent, it tells the crew medical officer and Earth.

    This page is a prototype. The readings are a simulated stream from fictional crew, because we have no real astronaut data.

    The problem

    On long missions there is no hospital. Astronauts are far from Earth, in a closed spacecraft with radiation, isolation and low gravity, and they carry much of the responsibility for spotting changes in their own health. They are also busy flying, maintaining and running experiments. A tool that needs data entry or only sounds alarms adds work and stress at the worst moment.

    How it meets the challenge

    The challenge asks for software that gathers health indicators and helps astronauts evaluate and act on them. Here is how each named risk is handled today in this prototype:

    Risk namedIn this prototype
    Cardiovascular eventsBlood pressure and heart rate
    RadiationDaily dose from a badge, with shelter guidance
    Behavioural health, isolationStress estimate, breathing guide, support suggestions
    Bone and muscle lossDaily strength-exercise tracking
    Immune changesNot covered yet (see next steps)

    Impact

    • Gives astronauts earlier, calmer warnings with something to do, which may reduce panic and delay.
    • Uses no crew time: readings arrive on their own.
    • Passes urgent cases to the medical officer and flight surgeons automatically.
    • Potential benefit on Earth: the same idea could support people far from hospitals. This is a possibility we would need to test, not something we have shown.

    What is creative about it

    • Calm by design. It never only says that something is wrong. Every message pairs the reading with what to do next.
    • Quiet when steady. It stays out of the way until you need it.
    • A breathing guide built into the status ring, to help with the stress that comes with a health scare.

    How we used design thinking

    1. Empathize. Astronauts are first responders for themselves, with little time and no hospital nearby.
    2. Define. Long-duration crews need to understand and act on their health without extra work or panic.
    3. Ideate. We first imagined an app where astronauts enter their numbers. Then we realised they would not have time, so we chose automatic sensing with quiet, calm guidance.
    4. Prototype. This page, with a simulated band.
    5. Test. Add: who tried it, what confused them, and what you changed

    Honest limits:

    • All readings are simulated, and the crew are fictional.
    • Ranges are illustrative. A real system would use NASA-approved medical ranges and clinical review.
    • Some signals, such as cuffless blood pressure, are hard to measure on the wrist today, so a real system would use the best sensor for each one.
    • It is decision support, not a doctor. Serious or unclear signs always go to the medical officer.

    Next steps

    • Learn each astronaut’s own normal, so alerts fit the person.
    • Track slow trends over weeks, such as sleep and bone-loading exercise.
    • Add immune-related indicators.
    • Review the guidance with a medical professional before any real use.
